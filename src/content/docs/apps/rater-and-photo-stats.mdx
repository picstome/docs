Two apps, both installed on your computer and both working directly on your
files. Neither one asks you to sign in to Picstome, and neither one uploads a
photo.

| App | What it does | Where to get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Picstome Rater** | Writes the favorites you marked in the panel as star ratings into your own files | [GitHub releases](https://github.com/picstome/picstome-rater/releases/latest) |
| **Photo Stats** | Reads the metadata of a folder and shows the gear and settings you really use | [picstome.com/photostats](https://picstome.com/photostats/) |

## Why you want them

Picstome knows **which** photos your client (or you) selected, but the selection
lives on the web. Your editor works on the files on your disk. These two apps are
the bridge between the two worlds, without exporting spreadsheets.

## Picstome Rater

The flow starts in the gallery, not in the app:

1. Open the gallery and go to the **Favorites** tab.
2. Press **Export favorites**.
3. In the **App** tab, press **Rate my images**.

That last button opens the Rater with the list of favorites already inside. In
the app you pick how many stars you want, confirm the folder where those photos
live, and press **Apply rating**. The app writes the rating into the files.

What it actually writes:

- the star rating in the **EXIF/XMP metadata**, not in a sidecar file;
- it looks for each photo by its file name, ignoring upper/lower case, so the
  folder has to contain the originals you uploaded;
- the rating is stored as the usual 0–100 % scale that Lightroom and Capture One
  read as stars.

So when you open that folder in Lightroom or Capture One, the favorites are
already rated and you can filter them without touching a mouse.

Things worth knowing:

- **It needs exiftool** installed on your computer. On macOS,
  `brew install exiftool`; on Windows, put `exiftool.exe` on your PATH. If the
  app cannot find it, it shows a setup message.
- **It remembers the last folder.** The next time you export favorites from the
  same shoot, you only confirm.
- **It never deletes or moves a photo.** It only writes metadata.
- **It is one-way.** Rating on the computer does not send anything back to
  Picstome; the stars live in your files, which is where your editor reads them.

## Photo Stats

Point it at a folder of photos and it answers questions about your own work:

- which camera and which lens you use most;
- the ISO, aperture, shutter speed and focal length you actually shoot at;
- how many frames are portrait or landscape;
- how your habits change over time.

Every chart filters the rest: click the 35 mm bar and the ISO chart updates to
show only your 35 mm photos.

It runs entirely on your machine. No account, no upload, no cloud, and it keeps
its index in a `photo_stats.db` file inside the folder you opened. The next scan
only reads what is new or changed.

It also needs **exiftool** to read the metadata, with the same install as the
Rater.

## Which one do I need?

- You deliver galleries and want your client's favorites as stars in Lightroom or
  Capture One: **Rater**.
- You are curious about your gear, your settings or your evolution as a
  photographer: **Photo Stats**.

Installing both is fine. They are small, they do not run in the background and
they never modify a photo beyond the star rating you ask for.
