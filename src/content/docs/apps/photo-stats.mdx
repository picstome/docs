## What it does

Photo Stats opens a folder of photos, reads every file's metadata and turns it into charts: which cameras and lenses show up most, at what ISO, aperture, shutter speed and focal length you shoot, and how all of it changes over the years.

It is not a gallery or an editor. It does not show the photos to rate them: it measures the pattern behind your work.

## How to use it

1. Open the app and choose **Open** to pick the photo folder.
2. Press **Scan**. The first pass indexes the files; later passes only read what is new or changed.
3. Use the filters on the left, or press a bar in a chart to narrow the rest.

Everything is computed on your computer. There is no account, nothing is uploaded and there is no cloud.

## What it shows

- **Cameras and lenses:** how many photos come from each body and each lens.
- **ISO, aperture, shutter speed and focal length:** the settings you actually shoot at.
- **Orientation:** how many photos are portrait and how many are landscape.
- **Timeline:** how your shooting changes month by month and year by year.
- **Cross-filtering:** press a bar and the rest of the charts update to show only that subset. Filter by 35 mm and the ISO chart shows the ISO of your 35 mm photos.

## Where the index lives

Photo Stats keeps a `photo_stats.db` file inside the folder you open. That file is the index; the next pass only reads what is new or modified, so it does not process thousands of photos again.

It needs **exiftool** to read metadata, with the same install as the [Rater](/apps/rater/): `brew install exiftool` on macOS, or `exiftool.exe` on the PATH on Windows.

## What it is useful for

- Deciding whether the lens you think you use is the one you actually use.
- Seeing whether new gear changed how you shoot or only the cupboard.
- Understanding your evolution without opening a spreadsheet.

Download it at [picstome.com/photostats](https://picstome.com/photostats/).
